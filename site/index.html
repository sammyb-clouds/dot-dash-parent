What’s a Dot Dash?

A small, hand-built gadget with a tiny screen, two arcade buttons, a buzzer and an LED. Tap a message out in Morse Code, or pick one of your own ready-made phrases, and send it to another device or phone.

Sending a message

  1. Tap it out.

    The front button codes — a short tap is a dot, a longer press is a dash. The device learns your own rhythm, so a slow tapper and a fast one are both read correctly. Hold the top button to send.

  2. It travels over Wi-Fi.

    A Dot Dash remembers up to five networks and uses whichever it finds, so it works at home, at a grandparent's and on holiday without being set up again. Messages go through Dot Dash's own servers, not anybody else's.

  3. It arrives.

    On another Dot Dash, where it can play out mark by mark like a telegraph, or in the Dot Dash app on a phone. Someone with neither can be sent a private web link and reply from that.

Why Morse code, and not a keyboard

Morse code is the whole alphabet elegantly built out of two symbols. It’s a simple logic and a satisfying puzzle. A few weeks of practice and you learn it for life, and the practice is the messaging — every message sent is a rep.

Train Mode helps you learn Morse code through simple lessons, and the device has the Morse key etched into the back panel.

The slowness is a feature. Morse is too slow to argue in and far too slow to doomscroll. What it is good at is the short, deliberate message you meant to send.
